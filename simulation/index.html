    Validation is part of ingest¶

    validate_elt collects every failure before raising, so a malformed table is fixed in one pass rather than one error at a time. Beyond the obvious range checks it enforces one bound worth stating explicitly:

    \[\sigma_i^2 \le \mu_i \,(E_i - \mu_i)\]

    For any loss distribution supported on \([0, E_i]\) with mean \(\mu_i\), the variance is maximised by the two-point distribution placing all its mass at the endpoints. A row exceeding that bound describes a distribution which cannot exist, so no severity model can honour it. Catching it at ingest is what stops the severity sampler from silently clipping the moments it was asked to reproduce.

    Frequency is modelled at the group level¶

    Both frequency models act on the annual count of a group of events — by default one group per peril — rather than on each event separately. The occurrences are then allocated to individual events in proportion to their rates.

    This matters for the negative binomial. Applied per event with dispersion \(k\), an event's count variance is \(\lambda_i + \lambda_i^2/k\). Real event rates are of order \(10^{-3}\), so the quadratic term is a millionth of the linear one and the portfolio is indistinguishable from Poisson: the over-dispersion has been specified and then thrown away.

    Applied to the group total, the marginal rates stay exact, since \(\mathbb{E}[N_i] = \mathbb{E}[N]\,p_i = \lambda_i\), while the shared draw of \(N\) induces

    \[\operatorname{Cov}(N_i, N_j) = \frac{\lambda_i \lambda_j}{k}\]

    between any two events in the group. That covariance is the clustering.

    The Poisson case needs no special handling: multinomial thinning of a Poisson total yields exactly independent \(\text{Poisson}(\lambda_i)\) per-event counts, so one code path serves both models.

    Secondary uncertainty is one family, read at three points¶

    With secondary_uncertainty=True, loss given occurrence is drawn from a method-of-moments fit on \([0, E_i]\) rather than fixed at \(\mu_i\). Writing \(m = \mu_i / E_i\) and \(s = \sigma_i / E_i\) for the damage ratio and its standard deviation:

    \[\nu = \frac{m\,(1 - m)}{s^2} - 1, \qquad \alpha = m\,\nu, \qquad \beta = (1 - m)\,\nu\]

    \(\nu\) is the concentration, and it is non-negative exactly when \(s^2 \le m\,(1 - m)\) — the feasibility bound enforced at ingest. Because validation runs first, the fit never has to clip a moment it was asked to reproduce.

    The probability mass at zero and at full exposure is not a pair of free parameters bolted onto the beta. It is the boundary member of the same family. As \(\nu \to 0\), the law \(\text{Beta}(m\nu, (1-m)\nu)\) converges to the Bernoulli law placing \(1 - m\) at \(0\) and \(m\) at \(1\), which is the unique distribution on \([0, 1]\) attaining the feasibility bound. So SeverityRegime reads one continuous family at three points:

    Regime When Fit
    DEGENERATE \(\sigma_i = 0\) All mass at \(\mu_i\)
    BETA \(0 < s^2 < m\,(1-m)\) Beta on \([0, E_i]\), continuous, no atom
    TWO_POINT \(s^2 = m\,(1-m)\) Mass \(1 - m\) at \(0\) and \(m\) at \(E_i\)

    This is a deliberate choice and worth stating plainly. A beta plus a free mass at each endpoint is the more familiar picture of secondary uncertainty, and it is what a location-level damage distribution really looks like. But an ELT row carries two moments, and two moments cannot determine four parameters. Any interior mass would come from a convention invented here rather than from the data, and it would propagate silently into every tail metric downstream. The two-parameter fit is exactly determined, reproduces both moments to machine precision in every regime, and still puts real mass on the endpoints wherever the moments demand it. Free endpoint masses belong with a richer input schema, not with this one.

    Two numerical points fall out of this, and both are tested.

    A row sitting exactly on the feasibility bound computes \(\nu\) as a few multiples of machine epsilon, of either sign. Testing \(\nu > 0\) would therefore label identical rows differently on the last bit, and a fit labelled BETA reports no endpoint mass for a distribution that in practice produces nothing else. The regime boundary is instead tested against the same relative slack validate_elt allows on the bound, so the two cannot drift apart.

    A std_loss that is positive but vanishing against exposure_value underflows the scaled variance and sends \(\nu\) to infinity. That is a DEGENERATE fit, not a TWO_POINT one: routing it to the endpoints would replace a variance of nearly zero with the largest the support allows.

    The AAL is unchanged by the flag, since the severity mean is \(\mu_i\) either way. What changes is everything above the first moment: the variance of the annual aggregate loss gains \(\sum_i \lambda_i \sigma_i^2\), and analytic_annual_loss_variance(..., secondary_uncertainty=True) is the closed form the simulation is checked against.

    Empty years are not optional¶

    A year in which nothing happened contributes no rows to the YELT, so the simulated year count cannot be recovered from the table and travels in yelt.attrs["n_years"]. to_ylt reindexes to every year in range(n_years).

    Collapsing a YELT with a bare groupby("sim_year") instead divides by the number of non-empty years and inflates the AAL by roughly

    \[\frac{1}{1 - e^{-\Lambda}}\]

    for a Poisson portfolio with total rate \(\Lambda\). There is a test asserting exactly that ratio against the naive computation, so the bug cannot return unnoticed.

    Performance¶

    The year loop is fully vectorised: counts are drawn per group in one call, and occurrences are assigned by inverse-CDF sampling against the cumulative rate vector. Uniforms are drawn in chunks to bound transient memory, and because every year's count is drawn before any uniform, results are identical for any chunk_years.

    def validate_elt(elt: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Validate an event loss table and return a normalised copy.

    Normalisation means canonical columns coerced to ``ELT_DTYPES`` and moved to
    the front in schema order, any extra columns preserved after them, and the
    index reset.

    The checks are: all canonical columns present; ``event_id`` integral,
    non-null and unique; ``rate`` finite and strictly positive; ``mean_loss`` and
    ``std_loss`` finite and non-negative; ``exposure_value`` finite and strictly
    positive; ``mean_loss`` at most ``exposure_value``; the variance bound below;
    and ``peril`` and ``region`` present and non-empty.

    The variance bound is the one that catches real ELTs. For any loss
    distribution supported on ``[0, exposure_value]`` with mean ``mean_loss``,
    the variance is maximised by the two-point distribution placing all its mass
    at the endpoints, giving ``mean_loss * (exposure_value - mean_loss)``. A row
    exceeding that describes a distribution which cannot exist, so no severity
    model can honour it. Catching it here rather than inside the sampler is what
    stops the secondary uncertainty module from silently clipping the moments it
    was asked to reproduce.

    Parameters
    ----------
    elt
        Candidate event loss table.

    Returns
    -------
    pandas.DataFrame
        A validated, normalised copy. The input is never modified.

    Raises
    ------
    ELTValidationError
        If any check fails. The message lists every failure, not just the first.

    Examples
    --------
    >>> import pandas as pd
    >>> from catagg import validate_elt
    >>> elt = pd.DataFrame(
    ...     {
    ...         "event_id": [1],
    ...         "rate": [0.5],
    ...         "mean_loss": [1.0e6],
    ...         "std_loss": [5.0e5],
    ...         "exposure_value": [5.0e6],
    ...         "peril": ["hail"],
    ...         "region": ["Plateau"],
    ...     }
    ... )
    >>> list(validate_elt(elt).columns)
    ['event_id', 'rate', 'mean_loss', 'std_loss', 'exposure_value', 'peril', 'region']
    """
    if not isinstance(elt, pd.DataFrame):  # pragma: no cover - defensive
        raise ELTValidationError(f"ELT must be a pandas DataFrame, got {type(elt).__name__}.")

    missing = [column for column in ELT_COLUMNS if column not in elt.columns]
    if missing:
        raise ELTValidationError(
            f"ELT is missing required column(s): {', '.join(missing)}. "
            f"Required schema is {', '.join(ELT_COLUMNS)}."
        )

    frame = elt.copy()
    problems: list[str] = []

    for column, dtype in ELT_DTYPES.items():
        try:
            frame[column] = frame[column].astype(pandas_dtype(dtype))
        except (TypeError, ValueError) as error:
            problems.append(f"column {column!r} cannot be cast to {dtype}: {error}")

    if problems:
        raise ELTValidationError("ELT failed validation:\n  - " + "\n  - ".join(problems))

    extra = [column for column in frame.columns if column not in ELT_COLUMNS]
    frame = frame[[*ELT_COLUMNS, *extra]].reset_index(drop=True)

    event_id = frame["event_id"]
    if event_id.isna().any():
        problems.append("event_id contains nulls")
    duplicated = event_id.duplicated(keep=False).to_numpy(dtype=bool)
    if bool(duplicated.any()):
        problems.append(f"event_id must be unique; duplicated: {_report(event_id, duplicated)}")

    for column in ("rate", "exposure_value"):
        values = frame[column].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)
        bad = ~np.isfinite(values) | (values <= 0.0)
        if bool(bad.any()):
            problems.append(
                f"{column} must be finite and strictly positive: {_report(event_id, bad)}"
            )

    for column in ("mean_loss", "std_loss"):
        values = frame[column].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)
        bad = ~np.isfinite(values) | (values < 0.0)
        if bool(bad.any()):
            problems.append(f"{column} must be finite and non-negative: {_report(event_id, bad)}")

    if not problems:
        mean = frame["mean_loss"].to_numpy()
        std = frame["std_loss"].to_numpy()
        exposure = frame["exposure_value"].to_numpy()

        over_exposed = mean > exposure
        if bool(over_exposed.any()):
            problems.append(
                f"mean_loss must not exceed exposure_value: {_report(event_id, over_exposed)}"
            )

        bound = mean * (exposure - mean)
        infeasible = (std**2 > bound * (1.0 + _VARIANCE_RTOL)) & ~over_exposed
        if bool(infeasible.any()):
            problems.append(
                "std_loss exceeds the largest standard deviation a distribution on "
                "[0, exposure_value] with this mean can have, "
                "sqrt(mean_loss * (exposure_value - mean_loss)): "
                f"{_report(event_id, infeasible)}"
            )

    for column in ("peril", "region"):
        labels = frame[column]
        bad = (labels.isna() | (labels.fillna("").str.strip() == "")).to_numpy(dtype=bool)
        if bool(bad.any()):
            problems.append(f"{column} must be a non-empty label: {_report(event_id, bad)}")

    if problems:
        raise ELTValidationError("ELT failed validation:\n  - " + "\n  - ".join(problems))

    return frame

    def read_elt(path: str | Path) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Read an event loss table from Parquet or CSV and validate it.

    The format is chosen from the file suffix: ``.parquet`` and ``.pq`` go
    through pyarrow, ``.csv`` through `pandas.read_csv`.

    Parameters
    ----------
    path
        Path to the file.

    Returns
    -------
    pandas.DataFrame
        A validated, normalised ELT.

    Raises
    ------
    ELTValidationError
        If the table does not satisfy the schema.
    ValueError
        If the suffix is not a recognised format.
    """
    path = Path(path)
    suffix = path.suffix.lower()
    if suffix in {".parquet", ".pq"}:
        frame = pd.read_parquet(path)
    elif suffix == ".csv":
        frame = pd.read_csv(path)
    else:
        raise ValueError(f"Unsupported ELT format {suffix!r}; expected .parquet or .csv.")
    return validate_elt(frame)

    catagg.frequency.models ¶

    Annual event-count models.

    Both models are defined on the annual count for a group of events, not on each event separately. This is deliberate, and it is the whole point of offering a negative binomial at all.

    If a negative binomial with dispersion k is applied to each event independently, that event's count has variance rate + rate ** 2 / k. Event rates in a real ELT are of order 1e-3, so the quadratic term is a millionth of the linear one and the portfolio behaves exactly as if it were Poisson: the over-dispersion has been specified and then thrown away.

    Applying the model to the group total and allocating occurrences to events in proportion to their rates keeps every marginal rate exact while making the aggregate count over-dispersed, which is what clustering means. See catagg.simulate.engine for the allocation step and for why the Poisson case reduces exactly to independent per-event Poisson draws.

    @dataclass(frozen=True)
class FrequencyModel:
    """Base class for annual event-count models.

    For validation purposes a model is fully described by
    `excess_variance_coefficient`: the constant ``c`` for which the annual count
    of a group with total rate ``Lambda`` has variance
    ``Lambda + c * Lambda ** 2``.
    """

    @property
    def excess_variance_coefficient(self) -> float:
        """Return the ``c`` in ``Var(N) = Lambda + c * Lambda ** 2``.

        Zero means Poisson.
        """
        raise NotImplementedError

    def annual_count_variance(self, group_rate: float) -> float:
        """Return the analytic variance of a group's annual event count.

        Parameters
        ----------
        group_rate
            Total annual rate of the group, ``sum(rate)``.

        Returns
        -------
        float
            The variance of the annual count.
        """
        return float(group_rate + self.excess_variance_coefficient * group_rate**2)

    def sample_annual_counts(
        self, group_rate: float, n_years: int, rng: Generator

        Parameters

            Total annual rate of the group, ``sum(rate)``.
        n_years
            Number of years to draw.
        rng
            Source of randomness.

        Returns
        -------
        numpy.ndarray
            Shape ``(n_years,)``, non-negative integer counts.
        """
        raise NotImplementedError

    catagg.severity.beta ¶

    Secondary uncertainty: the beta-with-point-mass severity model.

    An ELT row states a mean loss mu, a standard deviation sigma and an exposed value E. Loss given occurrence is then any distribution on [0, E] with those two moments. This module picks one by the method of moments, in the scaled variable X / E.

    Write m = mu / E and s = sigma / E. The method-of-moments beta is::

    nu = m * (1 - m) / s**2 - 1
alpha = m * nu
beta = (1 - m) * nu

    nu is the concentration alpha + beta, and it is non-negative exactly when s ** 2 <= m * (1 - m) — the feasibility bound catagg.validate_elt enforces on ingest. Because validation happens first, the fit never has to clip a moment it was asked to reproduce.

    Where the point masses come from

    The masses at 0 and at E are not free parameters bolted onto the beta. They are the boundary member of the same family. As nu -> 0 the law Beta(m * nu, (1 - m) * nu) converges to the Bernoulli law placing 1 - m at 0 and m at 1, which is the unique distribution on [0, 1] attaining the feasibility bound. So the three regimes in SeverityRegime are one continuous family read at three points: a degenerate spike when sigma = 0, an ordinary beta in the interior, and the two-point law on the endpoints when the variance sits at the bound.

    This is a deliberate choice, and it is worth stating plainly. A four-parameter family — a beta plus a free mass at each endpoint — is the more familiar picture of secondary uncertainty, and it is what a location-level damage distribution really looks like. But an ELT row carries two moments, and two moments cannot determine four parameters. Any interior mass would have to come from a convention invented here rather than from the data, and a convention invented here would propagate silently into every tail metric downstream. The two-parameter fit is exactly determined, reproduces both moments to machine precision in every regime, and still puts real mass on the endpoints wherever the moments actually demand it. Use BetaSeverity.endpoint_masses to see where that is. Free endpoint masses belong with a richer input schema, not with this one.

    Numerical note

    For small nu the beta places all but a vanishing share of its mass within exp(-1 / alpha) of an endpoint, which underflows float64 long before the regime boundary is reached. Draws in that range come back as exactly 0 or exactly E. That is float64 representing the fitted distribution faithfully, not the sampler failing, and the moments are unaffected.

class BetaSeverity:
    """A method-of-moments severity fit, one distribution per ELT row.

    Build one with `BetaSeverity.from_elt`. Every array is indexed by row
    position in the validated ELT, which is what `catagg.simulate` carries
    through the year loop.

    Attributes
    ----------
    mean_loss
        Target mean loss per event, in loss units.
    exposure_value
        Upper end of the support per event, in loss units.
    alpha
        Beta shape parameter ``alpha``. Zero outside `SeverityRegime.BETA`.
    beta
        Beta shape parameter ``beta``. Zero outside `SeverityRegime.BETA`.
    regime
        `SeverityRegime` code per event, as ``int8``.
    """

    mean_loss: NDArray[np.float64]
    exposure_value: NDArray[np.float64]
    alpha: NDArray[np.float64]
    beta: NDArray[np.float64]
    regime: NDArray[np.int8]

    def __len__(self) -> int:
        """Return the number of fitted events."""
        return int(self.mean_loss.shape[0])

    @classmethod
    def from_elt(cls, elt: pd.DataFrame) -> BetaSeverity:
        """Fit the severity model to every row of an event loss table.

        The ELT is validated first, so the feasibility bound holds and ``nu``
        cannot be meaningfully negative. `catagg.validate_elt` allows a
        relative slack of ``1e-9`` on that bound, so a row written by another
        tool in lower precision can arrive a hair above it; such a row is
        assigned to `SeverityRegime.TWO_POINT`, which reproduces its mean
        exactly and its variance to within that same relative slack.

        Parameters
        ----------
        elt
            Event loss table.

        Returns
        -------
        BetaSeverity
            The fit, with one entry per ELT row in ELT row order.

        Examples
        --------
        >>> import numpy as np
        >>> import catagg as ca
        >>> from catagg.severity import BetaSeverity
        >>> elt = ca.swiss_multiperil_elt(n_events=100, seed=0)
        >>> fit = BetaSeverity.from_elt(elt)
        >>> bool(np.allclose(fit.mean, elt["mean_loss"]))
        True
        """
        elt = validate_elt(elt)
        mean_loss = elt["mean_loss"].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)
        std_loss = elt["std_loss"].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)
        exposure_value = elt["exposure_value"].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)

        n_events = mean_loss.shape[0]
        alpha = np.zeros(n_events, dtype=np.float64)
        beta = np.zeros(n_events, dtype=np.float64)
        regime = np.full(n_events, SeverityRegime.DEGENERATE, dtype=np.int8)

        # nu is only defined where the variance is positive; everything else is
        # a spike at the mean and needs no shape parameters.
        stochastic = std_loss > 0.0
        positions = np.flatnonzero(stochastic)
        scaled_mean = mean_loss[stochastic] / exposure_value[stochastic]
        scaled_std = std_loss[stochastic] / exposure_value[stochastic]
        with np.errstate(divide="ignore", over="ignore", invalid="ignore"):
            nu = scaled_mean * (1.0 - scaled_mean) / scaled_std**2 - 1.0

        candidate_alpha = scaled_mean * nu
        candidate_beta = (1.0 - scaled_mean) * nu

        # Three outcomes. Two guards decide between them, and neither is
        # cosmetic.
        #
        # The concentration is compared against the validator's slack rather
        # than against zero. A row sitting exactly on the feasibility bound
        # computes nu as a few multiples of machine epsilon, of either sign, so
        # a test against zero would label identical rows differently on the last
        # bit - and a fit labelled BETA reports no endpoint mass for a
        # distribution that in practice produces nothing else. Snapping costs at
        # most `_BOUND_RTOL` of relative variance, which is precisely the
        # precision `validate_elt` has already declared it does not resolve.
        #
        # Non-finite shapes mean the scaled variance underflowed: std_loss is
        # positive but so small relative to exposure_value that float64 cannot
        # tell the distribution from a spike. That is a degenerate fit, not a
        # two-point one - routing it to the endpoints would replace a variance
        # of nearly zero with the largest variance the support allows.
        finite = np.isfinite(candidate_alpha) & np.isfinite(candidate_beta)
        interior = finite & (nu > _BOUND_RTOL) & (candidate_alpha > 0.0) & (candidate_beta > 0.0)
        endpoints = finite & ~interior

        alpha[positions[interior]] = candidate_alpha[interior]
        beta[positions[interior]] = candidate_beta[interior]
        regime[positions[interior]] = SeverityRegime.BETA
        regime[positions[endpoints]] = SeverityRegime.TWO_POINT

        return cls(
            mean_loss=mean_loss,
            exposure_value=exposure_value,
            alpha=alpha,
            beta=beta,
            regime=regime,
        )

    def raw_moment(self, order: int) -> NDArray[np.float64]:
        """Return the analytic raw moment ``E[X ** order]`` of each fit.

        Closed form, with no simulation involved. Orders one and two are what
        the fit was built to reproduce; higher orders are what calibrates the
        tolerance of a sampling test, in the same way
        `catagg.aal_standard_error` calibrates the AAL convergence test.

        For the beta regime the ``k``-th raw moment of ``X / E`` is the product
        of ``(alpha + r) / (alpha + beta + r)`` over ``r`` in ``0 .. k - 1``.
        For the two-point regime it is ``m``, for every order, because ``X`` is
        either ``0`` or ``E`` — which is also the limit of the beta product as
        the concentration goes to zero.

        Parameters
        ----------
        order
            Moment order, a strictly positive integer.

        Returns
        -------
        numpy.ndarray
            ``E[X ** order]`` per event, in loss units raised to ``order``.

        Raises
        ------
        ValueError
            If ``order`` is not a strictly positive integer.
        """
        if not isinstance(order, int) or order <= 0:
            raise ValueError(f"order must be a strictly positive integer, got {order!r}.")

        moment = self.mean_loss**order

        two_point = self.regime == SeverityRegime.TWO_POINT
        scaled_mean = np.where(two_point, self.mean_loss / self.exposure_value, 0.0)
        moment = np.where(two_point, scaled_mean * self.exposure_value**order, moment)

        is_beta = self.regime == SeverityRegime.BETA
        alpha = np.where(is_beta, self.alpha, 1.0)
        beta = np.where(is_beta, self.beta, 1.0)
        scaled = np.ones_like(alpha)
        for step in range(order):
            scaled *= (alpha + step) / (alpha + beta + step)
        return np.asarray(
            np.where(is_beta, scaled * self.exposure_value**order, moment), dtype=np.float64
        )

    @property
    def mean(self) -> NDArray[np.float64]:
        """Return the analytic mean of each fit, which must equal ``mean_loss``."""
        return self.raw_moment(1)

    @property
    def std(self) -> NDArray[np.float64]:
        """Return the analytic standard deviation, which must equal ``std_loss``."""
        variance = self.raw_moment(2) - self.raw_moment(1) ** 2
        return np.sqrt(np.maximum(variance, 0.0))

    def endpoint_masses(self) -> tuple[NDArray[np.float64], NDArray[np.float64]]:
        """Return the probability mass at zero and at ``exposure_value``.

        Both are zero in `SeverityRegime.BETA`, where the fitted law is
        continuous. They are the whole distribution in `SeverityRegime.TWO_POINT`,
        and a single unit mass in `SeverityRegime.DEGENERATE` when the mean sits
        on an endpoint. See the module docstring for why they are not free
        parameters.

        Returns
        -------
        tuple of numpy.ndarray
            ``(mass at zero, mass at exposure_value)``, per event.
        """
        two_point = self.regime == SeverityRegime.TWO_POINT
        degenerate = self.regime == SeverityRegime.DEGENERATE

        scaled_mean = np.where(two_point, self.mean_loss / self.exposure_value, 0.0)
        at_zero = np.where(two_point, 1.0 - scaled_mean, 0.0)
        at_exposure = np.where(two_point, scaled_mean, 0.0)

        at_zero = np.where(degenerate & (self.mean_loss == 0.0), 1.0, at_zero)
        at_exposure = np.where(

def from_elt(cls, elt: pd.DataFrame) -> BetaSeverity:
    """Fit the severity model to every row of an event loss table.

    The ELT is validated first, so the feasibility bound holds and ``nu``
    cannot be meaningfully negative. `catagg.validate_elt` allows a
    relative slack of ``1e-9`` on that bound, so a row written by another
    tool in lower precision can arrive a hair above it; such a row is
    assigned to `SeverityRegime.TWO_POINT`, which reproduces its mean
    exactly and its variance to within that same relative slack.

    Parameters
    ----------
    elt
        Event loss table.

    Returns
    -------
    BetaSeverity
        The fit, with one entry per ELT row in ELT row order.

    Examples
    --------
    >>> import numpy as np
    >>> import catagg as ca
    >>> from catagg.severity import BetaSeverity
    >>> elt = ca.swiss_multiperil_elt(n_events=100, seed=0)
    >>> fit = BetaSeverity.from_elt(elt)
    >>> bool(np.allclose(fit.mean, elt["mean_loss"]))
    True
    """
    elt = validate_elt(elt)
    mean_loss = elt["mean_loss"].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)
    std_loss = elt["std_loss"].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)
    exposure_value = elt["exposure_value"].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)

    n_events = mean_loss.shape[0]
    alpha = np.zeros(n_events, dtype=np.float64)
    beta = np.zeros(n_events, dtype=np.float64)
    regime = np.full(n_events, SeverityRegime.DEGENERATE, dtype=np.int8)

    # nu is only defined where the variance is positive; everything else is
    # a spike at the mean and needs no shape parameters.
    stochastic = std_loss > 0.0
    positions = np.flatnonzero(stochastic)
    scaled_mean = mean_loss[stochastic] / exposure_value[stochastic]
    scaled_std = std_loss[stochastic] / exposure_value[stochastic]
    with np.errstate(divide="ignore", over="ignore", invalid="ignore"):
        nu = scaled_mean * (1.0 - scaled_mean) / scaled_std**2 - 1.0

    candidate_alpha = scaled_mean * nu
    candidate_beta = (1.0 - scaled_mean) * nu

    # Three outcomes. Two guards decide between them, and neither is
    # cosmetic.
    #
    # The concentration is compared against the validator's slack rather
    # than against zero. A row sitting exactly on the feasibility bound
    # computes nu as a few multiples of machine epsilon, of either sign, so
    # a test against zero would label identical rows differently on the last
    # bit - and a fit labelled BETA reports no endpoint mass for a
    # distribution that in practice produces nothing else. Snapping costs at
    # most `_BOUND_RTOL` of relative variance, which is precisely the
    # precision `validate_elt` has already declared it does not resolve.
    #
    # Non-finite shapes mean the scaled variance underflowed: std_loss is
    # positive but so small relative to exposure_value that float64 cannot
    # tell the distribution from a spike. That is a degenerate fit, not a
    # two-point one - routing it to the endpoints would replace a variance
    # of nearly zero with the largest variance the support allows.
    finite = np.isfinite(candidate_alpha) & np.isfinite(candidate_beta)
    interior = finite & (nu > _BOUND_RTOL) & (candidate_alpha > 0.0) & (candidate_beta > 0.0)
    endpoints = finite & ~interior

    alpha[positions[interior]] = candidate_alpha[interior]
    beta[positions[interior]] = candidate_beta[interior]
    regime[positions[interior]] = SeverityRegime.BETA
    regime[positions[endpoints]] = SeverityRegime.TWO_POINT

    return cls(
        mean_loss=mean_loss,
        exposure_value=exposure_value,
        alpha=alpha,
        beta=beta,
        regime=regime,
    )

    catagg.simulate.engine ¶

    The year loop: ELT in, year event loss table out.

    Occurrences are generated in two stages per event group.

    1. Draw the group's annual count N from the frequency model.
    2. Allocate each of those N occurrences to an event, independently, with probability proportional to the event's rate.

    For the Poisson case this is exactly equivalent to drawing each event's count from an independent Poisson(rate_i): Poisson thinning is exact, not an approximation. For the negative binomial it keeps every marginal rate exact, since E[N_i] = E[N] * p_i = rate_i, while making the group total over-dispersed, because the shared draw of N induces a positive covariance rate_i * rate_j / k between any two events in the group. That covariance is the clustering; per-event negative binomial draws would not produce it.

    A third stage then attaches a loss to each occurrence. With secondary_uncertainty=False that loss is the event's mean_loss, which leaves the AAL exact and understates every other moment. With True it is a draw from catagg.severity.BetaSeverity, which leaves the AAL exact as well — the severity mean is unchanged — and adds sum(rate * std_loss ** 2) to the variance of the annual aggregate loss.

        occurrence of an event carries the same loss, which understates every
        moment above the first.

        to each group's annual total. ``None`` treats the whole ELT as one group.

    pandas.DataFrame

        ``gross_loss``, sorted by ``sim_year`` then ``occurrence_index``, with
        ``attrs["n_years"]`` set. Years with no occurrences contribute no rows.

    >>> list(yelt.columns)
    ['sim_year', 'event_id', 'occurrence_index', 'gross_loss']
    """

        raise ValueError(f"n_years must be strictly positive, got {n_years}.")
    if chunk_years <= 0:
        raise ValueError(f"chunk_years must be strictly positive, got {chunk_years}.")

    elt = validate_elt(elt)
    model = resolve_frequency(frequency, dispersion)
    groups = _group_positions(elt, group_by)

    rate = elt["rate"].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)
    event_id = elt["event_id"].to_numpy(dtype=np.int64)
    mean_loss = elt["mean_loss"].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)

    # One child per group, plus one reserved for severity. Spawning is ordered,
    # so the group streams are the ones they would have been without the extra
    # child: adding severity does not move a run with it switched off.
    seeds = np.random.SeedSequence(seed).spawn(len(groups) + 1)
    total_counts = np.zeros(n_years, dtype=np.int64)
    year_pieces: list[NDArray[np.int32]] = []
    position_pieces: list[NDArray[np.int64]] = []

        rng = np.random.default_rng(child)
        group_rates = rate[positions]
        counts = model.sample_annual_counts(float(group_rates.sum()), n_years, rng)
        total_counts += counts

        within_group = _allocate_occurrences(group_rates, counts, rng, chunk_years)
        position_pieces.append(positions[within_group])
        year_pieces.append(np.repeat(np.arange(n_years, dtype=np.int32), counts))

    occurrence_years = np.concatenate(year_pieces) if year_pieces else np.empty(0, dtype=np.int32)

        np.concatenate(position_pieces) if position_pieces else np.empty(0, dtype=np.int64)
    )

    order = np.argsort(occurrence_years, kind="stable")
    occurrence_positions = occurrence_positions[order]

    occurrence_index = (
        np.arange(n_occurrences, dtype=np.int64) - np.repeat(starts, total_counts)
    ).astype(np.int32, copy=False)

    if secondary_uncertainty:
        gross_loss = BetaSeverity.from_elt(elt).sample(
            occurrence_positions, np.random.default_rng(seeds[-1])
        )
    else:
        gross_loss = mean_loss[occurrence_positions]

        {
            "sim_year": np.repeat(np.arange(n_years, dtype=np.int32), total_counts),

            "gross_loss": gross_loss,
        }
    ).astype(YELT_DTYPES)
    yelt.attrs["n_years"] = int(n_years)

    """Collapse a YELT to one row per simulated year.

    Every year in ``range(n_years)`` appears, including the ones in which nothing
    happened. Those years carry zero loss and are not optional: dropping them, as
    a bare ``groupby("sim_year")`` silently does, divides by the number of
    *non-empty* years and inflates the AAL, by a factor of roughly
    ``1 / (1 - exp(-Lambda))`` for a Poisson portfolio.

    Parameters
    ----------
    yelt
        Year event loss table.
    n_years
        Explicit year count if ``yelt.attrs`` no longer carries it.

    Returns
    -------
    pandas.DataFrame
        A YLT with columns ``sim_year``, ``aggregate_loss`` and
        ``max_occurrence_loss``.
    """
    resolved = n_years_of(yelt, n_years)
    years = np.arange(resolved, dtype=np.int32)
    sim_year = yelt["sim_year"].to_numpy(dtype=np.int64)
    gross_loss = yelt["gross_loss"].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)

    aggregate = np.bincount(sim_year, weights=gross_loss, minlength=resolved)
    maximum = np.zeros(resolved, dtype=np.float64)
    np.maximum.at(maximum, sim_year, gross_loss)

    ylt = pd.DataFrame(
        {"sim_year": years, "aggregate_loss": aggregate, "max_occurrence_loss": maximum}
    )
    ylt.attrs["n_years"] = resolved
    return ylt

    def analytic_annual_loss_variance(
    elt: pd.DataFrame,
    frequency: str | FrequencyModel = "poisson",
    dispersion: float | None = None,
    *,
    group_by: str | None = "peril",
    secondary_uncertainty: bool = False,
) -> float:
    """Return the variance of the annual aggregate loss, in closed form.

    For each frequency group with excess-variance coefficient ``c``::

        Var = sum(rate * std_loss ** 2)
            + sum(rate * mean_loss ** 2)
            + c * sum(rate * mean_loss) ** 2

    The first term is present only when severity is stochastic. The middle term
    is the usual compound-Poisson contribution. The last is the clustering
    contribution, quadratic in the group's expected loss, which is what makes an
    over-dispersed frequency matter in the tail. Groups are assumed independent,
    which is what the engine currently does.

    Parameters
    ----------
    elt
        Event loss table.
    frequency
        Frequency model, as passed to `catagg.simulate`.
    dispersion
        Negative binomial shape parameter ``k``, if applicable.
    group_by
        Column defining the frequency groups, matching the simulation.
    secondary_uncertainty
        Include the ``sum(rate * std_loss ** 2)`` severity term. Leave ``False``
        to match a simulation run with degenerate severity.

    Returns
    -------
    float
        The variance of the annual aggregate loss.

    Raises
    ------
    CataggError
        If ``group_by`` is not a column of the ELT.
    """
    elt = validate_elt(elt)
    model = resolve_frequency(frequency, dispersion)
    coefficient = model.excess_variance_coefficient

    if group_by is None:
        labels: pd.Series = pd.Series(np.zeros(len(elt), dtype=np.int8), index=elt.index)
    elif group_by in elt.columns:
        labels = elt[group_by]
    else:
        raise CataggError(f"group_by={group_by!r} is not a column of the ELT.")

    rate = elt["rate"].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)
    mean = elt["mean_loss"].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)
    std = elt["std_loss"].to_numpy(dtype=np.float64)

    variance = float(np.dot(rate, mean**2))
    if secondary_uncertainty:
        variance += float(np.dot(rate, std**2))

    for label in labels.unique():
        mask = (labels == label).to_numpy()
        variance += coefficient * float(np.dot(rate[mask], mean[mask])) ** 2
    return variance

    def swiss_multiperil_elt(
    n_events: int = 1_000,
    seed: int | None = 0,
    perils: tuple[PerilSpec, ...] = SWISS_PERILS,
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Generate a synthetic multi-peril event loss table.

    Event mean losses are lognormal; rates are proportional to a negative power
    of the mean loss and then rescaled so each peril's rates sum exactly to its
    ``annual_rate``, which makes the analytic AAL of the result a clean
    reference value. ``std_loss`` is a fixed multiple of ``mean_loss``, clipped
    to stay strictly inside the bound enforced by `catagg.validate_elt`.

    Parameters
    ----------
    n_events
        Total number of events across all perils.
    seed
        Seed for reproducibility. ``None`` draws from OS entropy.
    perils
        Peril specifications.

    Returns
    -------
    pandas.DataFrame
        A valid ELT with the canonical columns.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        If ``n_events`` is not strictly positive.
    """
    if n_events <= 0:
        raise ValueError(f"n_events must be strictly positive, got {n_events}.")

    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    frames: list[pd.DataFrame] = []
    next_event_id = 1

    for spec in perils:
        size = max(1, round(n_events * spec.share_of_events))
        mean_loss = rng.lognormal(mean=spec.log_mean, sigma=spec.log_sigma, size=size)

        weights = mean_loss**-spec.rate_severity_exponent
        rate = spec.annual_rate * weights / weights.sum()

        exposure_value = mean_loss * spec.exposure_multiple
        max_std = np.sqrt(mean_loss * (exposure_value - mean_loss))
        std_loss = np.minimum(spec.coefficient_of_variation * mean_loss, _VARIANCE_SAFETY * max_std)

        frames.append(
            pd.DataFrame(
                {
                    "event_id": np.arange(next_event_id, next_event_id + size, dtype=np.int64),
                    "rate": rate,
                    "mean_loss": mean_loss,
                    "std_loss": std_loss,
                    "exposure_value": exposure_value,
                    "peril": spec.name,
                    "region": rng.choice(np.array(_REGIONS), size=size),
                }
            )
        )
        next_event_id += size

    from catagg.io.elt import validate_elt

    return validate_elt(pd.concat(frames, ignore_index=True))
